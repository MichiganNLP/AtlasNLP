Key Findings

What the Data Reveals

Five findings from AtlasNLP — aggregated from 18,035 datasets across the Core set and 1,661 entries in the Gold set.

Finding 1

NLP Dataset Production Is Geographically Concentrated

39%

of all datasets are concentrated in the top 5 countries by content coverage.

A small number of countries — primarily the United States, China, and a handful of European nations — dominate NLP dataset production. This concentration is visible across every task category.

Finding 2

Task Coverage Is Uneven Across the Atlas

Certain task categories — especially machine translation, language modeling, and named entity recognition — attract far more datasets than others. Many specialized tasks relevant to low-resource contexts are underrepresented.

Finding 3

Producer and Content Countries Often Diverge

A dataset about a country's language is not necessarily produced by researchers from that country. AtlasNLP tracks both content countries (whose language or community is covered) and producer countries (where the authors are based).

For many nations in Africa, Southeast Asia, and South America — content coverage exceeds producer presence, meaning their languages are studied largely by outsiders.

Finding 4

Most NLP Datasets Are Monolingual

75%

of Core datasets target a single language, with no cross-lingual scope.

Despite growing interest in multilingual NLP, the field remains dominated by monolingual datasets. Multilingual datasets are disproportionately produced by high-resource countries and tend to include English as a primary language, reinforcing the geographic skew documented in other findings.

Finding 5

Country Attribution Relies on Multiple Evidence Tiers

33.8%

of datasets lack recoverable country attribution through any method.

AtlasNLP uses a cascade of attribution methods: explicit metadata, language-to-country inference, author affiliation, and URL domain inference. A significant portion of datasets could not be attributed to any country even with all methods applied.
